# Ars Imaginis Privacy Policy

Effective October 19, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Ars Imaginis (“the app”) handles information. The app is made and offered by Alyssa I. Agard, an individual developer in New Jersey, United States (“the developer”).

In short: the app does not collect, store, sell, or share your personal information. It has no accounts, analytics, advertising, or tracking, and it does not connect to the internet. Everything it does with your camera, photos, and sensors happens on your iPhone.

## Who is responsible

The developer is responsible for this policy and, under European and United Kingdom law, is the controller of the limited information described in “Information the developer may receive.” You can reach the developer at appstore@alyssaagard.dev.

## Information the app does not collect

The app does not collect or send your name, contact details, location, device identifiers, usage data, diagnostics, photos, camera images, face data, or any other personal information. It contains no third-party code for analytics, advertising, or crash reporting, and it never connects to the internet.

## Camera and on-device analysis

The app uses the camera to show the viewfinder and to take photos.

To give framing and lighting guidance, the app analyzes the live camera image on your iPhone with Apple’s Vision framework. Depending on the subject you choose, it looks for the positions of faces and bodies, or of animals, and for the brightness of the scene. Each analysis happens in memory, is discarded within a fraction of a second, and is never saved or sent anywhere.

## Biometric information

The app locates faces and features such as the eyes in the live image only to give framing advice. It does not create a face template, faceprint, or any measurement used to identify or recognize a person, and it does not store any face data. Nothing leaves your device, and the developer never receives or has access to any face data.

Because the app does not collect biometric identifiers as defined by laws such as the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act, the Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act, Washington’s biometric privacy law, and the Colorado Privacy Act, it keeps no biometric data and no retention schedule for it. If that ever changes, this policy will be updated and your consent will be asked first.

## Motion sensors

The app reads your iPhone’s motion sensors to show the level indicator and to tell when the camera is steady. These readings are used only on your device and are not saved.

## Photo library

The app asks for permission to add photos to your photo library. This permission only lets the app add new photos. The app cannot see, read, or change anything already in your library.

Photos are saved exactly as your iPhone’s camera system produces them. Each photo includes the standard camera information that iOS stores in it, such as the device model, lens, exposure settings, and the date and time. If you use Portrait or turn on Save depth, depth information is stored inside the photo so it can be used for portrait editing. If you use Portrait or turn on Save a styled copy, a second photo with the background blurred or your chosen look is saved as well. The app does not add location information to photos.

## Temporary in-app gallery

So you can review your shots, the app keeps copies of the photos you take during the current session in its private temporary storage on your iPhone. Only the app can reach this storage. The copies are deleted when the app closes or the next time you open it, and iOS may also clear them at any time.

If you have not allowed photo library access, your photos exist only in this temporary storage and are lost when the app closes. If you allow access while the app is still open, the photos still in the gallery are saved to your library automatically.

## Preferences stored on your device

The app remembers your preferences on your iPhone, such as your color theme, appearance, lens choices, look settings, camera settings when Remember Settings is on, whether you have seen the welcome screens, and which version of the Terms of Use and this policy you agreed to. These stay on your device and are removed when you delete the app.

## Information the developer may receive

Email. If you write to the developer, the developer receives your email address and whatever you include in your message. It is used only to reply to you and is kept only as long as needed for that, and no longer than two years after the conversation ends unless the law requires otherwise.

TestFlight. If you test the app through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple may share with the developer the name and email address you were invited with, your device model and iOS version, crash reports, and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. This is used only to test and improve the app and is handled under Apple’s terms.

App Store. If you choose in your iPhone’s settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may share aggregated, de-identified usage and crash statistics. The developer cannot identify you from them.

## Selling, sharing, and advertising

The developer does not sell personal information, does not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, does not use it for targeted advertising or profiling, and does not make automated decisions about you. Information is disclosed only if the law requires it.

## Tracking, Do Not Track, and Global Privacy Control

The app does not track you across apps or websites, so it never shows Apple’s App Tracking Transparency prompt. Because there is no tracking, there is nothing for Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signals to turn off, and you are treated as having opted out of any sale or sharing.

## Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, or under 16 where local law sets a higher age, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent the developer personal information, write to appstore@alyssaagard.dev and it will be deleted.

## Security

Because the app keeps everything on your iPhone, your information is protected by iOS security, including your device passcode and encryption. The developer uses reasonable measures to protect the few emails and TestFlight records described above.

## Your privacy rights

Wherever you live, you can ask the developer what personal information it holds about you, ask for a copy, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, and ask how it is used. Write to appstore@alyssaagard.dev. The developer may ask you to confirm your request from the same email address, will answer within 30 days or sooner when the law requires, and will never treat you differently for using your rights.

## California residents

Whether or not the California Consumer Privacy Act applies to the developer, California residents have the right to know, delete, and correct personal information, to opt out of its sale or sharing, to limit the use of sensitive personal information, and not to be discriminated against for using these rights.

In the past 12 months the developer has collected only identifiers (an email address, and for TestFlight testers a name), the contents of messages, and diagnostic data from Apple, and only from people who wrote to the developer or tested the app. None of it was sold or shared, and no sensitive personal information was collected. You may use an authorized agent to make a request.

Under California’s “Shine the Light” law, the developer does not disclose personal information to third parties for their direct marketing. This policy is also provided under the California Online Privacy Protection Act.

## Colorado, Virginia, Connecticut, and other U.S. states

Residents of Colorado, Virginia, Connecticut, Utah, Texas, Oregon, Montana, and other states with consumer privacy laws have the rights to access, correct, delete, and get a portable copy of their personal data, and to opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling. The developer does none of those last three.

If the developer declines your request, you may appeal by replying to the decision with the word “Appeal.” The developer will answer within 45 days. If you are not satisfied, you may contact your state’s Attorney General.

You have the rights to access, rectify, erase, restrict, and port your personal data, to object to its use, and to complain to your data protection authority, such as your national authority in the European Union, the Information Commissioner’s Office in the United Kingdom, or the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner in Switzerland.
